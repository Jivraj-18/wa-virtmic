E4 · Google Meet agent "Maya"

PARTIAL

1The idea

Maya sits in a Google Meet as one more participant (joining as a guest, or with her own Google account). She listens silently and keeps a live transcript. She speaks only when someone addresses her by name ("Maya, what do you think?"), and goes quiet at once when someone talks over her. Every few minutes at most, she may post a short suggestion in the Meet chat ("Maybe consider X", "You seem stuck on Y").

Before building, we compared open-source meeting bots (joinly, Attendee, meeto, Vexa). They all drive a real Chrome on meet.google.com with virtual audio devices, and most ship as gigabyte-sized Docker stacks. We reuse our own Chrome + PulseAudio + gpt-realtime stack from the WhatsApp agent and borrow only their Meet selector patterns. Details are in experiments/E4/RESULTS.md.

2How it works

gpt-realtime hears the whole meeting, but automatic replies are switched off (turn_detection.create_response=false). Each utterance's transcript runs through a small rule set: "Maya, …", "Hey Maya …", "…, Maya?" count as addressed; "Maya's numbers", "ask Maya later", "sent it to Maya" don't. The rule fires on the streaming partial transcript when the name comes first, so Maya starts answering before the final transcript arrives. A local energy VAD on the meeting audio cuts her off within a fraction of a second when anyone talks over her. Maya's Chrome, port and audio devices are all separate from the WhatsApp setup.

4Results

Two scripted meetings, run against Maya on the harness's private devices. Most lines are not for Maya, including people addressing others by name ("Maria, can you…", "Ravi, what do you think…") and third-person mentions ("Let's ask Maya to…"). Three lines per meeting address her, and in each meeting one participant talks over her long answer. Answer latency runs from the end of the speaker's voice to Maya's first audible sound.

5What worked, what didn't, what's next

Worked

  • No false triggers. Maya stayed silent on every line not meant for her, including other people addressed by name and "ask Maya…" mentions.
  • She answered every line addressed to her, in about 2 s, using what had been said earlier in the meeting.
  • Talk-over stops her in about 0.15 s (the WhatsApp agent in E0 took 1.5 s), thanks to a local energy VAD.
  • Chat suggestions were on topic and rate-limited.
  • The Meet join, chat and leave code passed against a local mock Meet page in headless Chrome.

Didn't / not yet

  • Not yet tested in a real Google Meet. That only needs a meeting link: Maya joins as a guest and the host admits her.
  • The Meet selectors are best-effort and were checked only against a mock page. Meet changes its DOM often.
  • The local VAD will also stop Maya for coughs or background noise. In a real meeting the threshold may need tuning.
  • Some suggestions repeat a theme. Once, a suggestion promised a task ("I'll send the checklist"); the prompt now forbids that.
  • There's no speaker diarisation: Maya doesn't know who said what.

Next

  • Real Meet test with the user: log in, join, address Maya, talk over her, check that chat tips appear.
  • Read the active speaker's name from the Meet DOM, so the transcript and answers can name people.
  • Ignore short noises for barge-in: require speech-like energy for about 250 ms, or confirm with the server VAD.

6How to run it

# unit tests (addressing rules, suggestion rate limit, Meet control on a mock page in headless Chrome)
python3 tests/test_meet_addressing.py
python3 tests/test_meet_suggester.py
python3 tests/test_meet_browser.py

# simulated meeting with the E0 harness (no Meet needed)
python3 scripts/sim_call.py experiments/E4/scripts/meeting.yaml --tag E4 --greeting-timeout 3 \
  --agent "{python} -u -m scripts.meet.maya agent --in-device {in_device} --out-device {out_device} \
           --transcript {transcript} --seconds {seconds} --suggest-first 30 --suggest-interval 45"
python3 -m scripts.meet.score_sim experiments/runs/E4_meeting_<ts> experiments/E4/scripts/meeting.yaml

# real Meet (needs a meeting link; Maya joins as a guest named "Maya", the host admits her)
# `maya login` is optional: only if you want her on a Google account instead of as a guest
python3 -m scripts.meet.maya devices up      # maya_out, maya_mic_sink, maya_mic
python3 -m scripts.meet.maya login           # Maya's Chrome on the noVNC desktop -> log in by hand
python3 -m scripts.meet.maya join https://meet.google.com/xxx-yyyy-zzz
python3 -m scripts.meet.maya probe           # if a button isn't found: list what Meet shows
python3 -m scripts.meet.maya leave

Run output: maya_events.jsonl (when Maya was addressed, barge-ins, chat suggestions) next to the transcript. Real meetings write to ~/maya_meetings/<ts>/, outside the repo.
